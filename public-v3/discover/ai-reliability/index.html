AI Reliability Guide — Know What to Verify

Question certainty. Trace the evidence. Protect information. Make decisions with appropriate human review.

Human-directed · In development

Verify material facts

For legal, financial, medical, safety or consequential decisions, consult qualified sources and appropriate professionals. Open any citations and check their relevance and dates.

Recognize missing evidence

An AI response might contain outdated information, false precision, fabricated references or omitted caveats. Ask what is known, what is inferred and what remains uncertain.

Protect personal information

Avoid sharing passwords, sensitive identification, confidential business data or private records without understanding a service's privacy and retention policies. Product privacy controls must be checked rather than assumed.

Keep humans in control

AI-generated plans and recommendations should be reviewed before being acted upon. Ad Astra's verification and meta-evaluation features are in internal development and are not guaranteed to eliminate errors.

A repeatable four-step fact check

1. Isolate one checkable claim in plain language. 2. Find the original, dated evidence rather than a summary of a summary. 3. Compare at least one independent source, noting scope, methods and contradictions. 4. Record what is established, what remains uncertain, and the next check needed before acting. A plausible response without verifiable evidence is a lead, not a conclusion.

Worked example: a technology-lifespan claim

Suppose a draft answer says, 'This new energy device lasts 30 years.' Treat that as a hypothetical claim, not a product fact. Look for a specific model and revision, the manufacturer’s stated test conditions, third-party durability data, the difference between a warranty and a performance forecast, and how the estimate changes with temperature, duty cycle and maintenance. If the evidence is absent, say that the lifespan is unverified.

Separate source evidence from generated suggestions

A suggested plan is not a cited finding. For every important factual sentence, capture the exact claim, the source title or owner, a working source link, its publication or revision date, and the passage or data that supports it. Open links yourself; a citation-like string does not prove the source exists. Record conflicting evidence alongside supporting evidence, and never manufacture references.

A decision record you can reuse

Copy this worksheet locally: QUESTION — What am I deciding? CLAIM — What specifically must be true? EVIDENCE — What primary source supports it? DATE — When was it valid? UNCERTAINTY — What could invalidate it? IMPACT — What happens if it is wrong? NEXT CHECK — What independent verification will I perform? DECISION — Proceed, test further, or hold? Do not put confidential facts in an unapproved AI service.

What the private Ad Astra prototype does today

The five protected workspace modes (Ask, Research, Create, Learn and Explore) can prepare structured briefs using deterministic rules. They do not perform live source retrieval, validate references or run a language model. Workspace text stays in your browser until you intentionally choose the private backend action; that action transmits the selected task to a stateless server and is not appropriate for secrets or regulated personal information. These are prototype boundaries, not a general privacy guarantee.

When to seek qualified review

For medical, legal, financial, security or other high-impact decisions, use current primary sources and qualified professionals rather than treating this guide or an AI output as personalized advice. A useful sign of progress is a shorter list of unsupported claims and a clearly documented next verification step, not an unearned confidence score.

AD ASTRA is developing an informational, human-directed intelligence experience. Conceptual diagrams are not operational AI systems or substitute for expert review.
