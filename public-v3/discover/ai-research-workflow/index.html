A Practical AI-Assisted Research Workflow

Plan research questions, collect evidence and produce a conclusion that shows what is known and what is uncertain.

Human-directed · In development

Plan research questions, collect evidence and produce a conclusion that shows what is known and what is uncertain.

Define the decision

Replace an open-ended topic with a concrete question. For example, 'Which of these two software options best fits a small team with five editors and a fixed monthly budget?' Write down what would change your decision.

Decide what counts as evidence

For product specifications, seek official documentation. For pricing, verify current public terms. For scientific claims, examine appropriate original research and reputable syntheses. A list of search snippets alone is not evidence.

Build an evidence table

Record each claim, source name, source date, the passage that supports it and whether there are contradictions. Avoid copying conclusions without their limits or qualifiers.

Compare like with like

Use the same criteria for every option. Separate objective facts from subjective preferences, and label illustrative calculations or assumptions rather than treating them as discovered facts.

Produce a useful outcome

End with a short answer, key supporting evidence, important uncertainty and actions that would reduce remaining risk. Keep enough source detail that someone else could check the work.

Repeat verification when facts change

Research involving prices, availability, deadlines and new developments becomes stale. Recheck time-sensitive claims close to the final decision.
