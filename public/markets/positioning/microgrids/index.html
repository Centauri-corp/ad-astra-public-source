AD ASTRA / INFRASTRUCTURE RESEARCH

Intelligence meets
local energy.

A proposed architecture for pairing governed compute with local generation, storage and human-supervised control. This is a research direction, not an operational micro-grid, AGI or ASI offer.

Concept only — not for saleNo autonomous energy dispatch

The proposed stack

Separable systems with independent safety gates

COGNITIVE LAYER

Forecasts and reviewable decisions

Explore demand forecasts, scenario planning and operator-facing explanations. No model is authorized to dispatch power, trade energy or override protection equipment.

ENERGY LAYER

Generation, storage and controls

Explore solar, wind, batteries, utility interconnection and edge compute as site-specific options. Licensed engineering, grid studies and safety systems would remain primary.

Regional value hypotheses

B2B and B2C research, not service claims

01 / NORTH AMERICA

Resilience planning

Enterprise and industrial

Investigate whether site-level energy forecasts could improve continuity planning for critical compute workloads.

Residential and prosumer

Investigate household energy visibility and locally hosted assistance where approved hardware exists.

Hardware and offtake

Test equipment leasing or a service contract against signed demand, site cost and utility requirements; no deployment timeline is promised.

Proof gate: site design, utility interconnection, measured outage performance, hardware warranty and billing authority.

02 / EUROPE + UK

Governance and carbon evidence

Enterprise and public sector

Investigate auditable demand flexibility and energy reporting under local legal and procurement requirements.

Community and residential

Investigate shared-energy information with clear privacy and consent boundaries.

Hardware and offtake

Evaluate utility or municipal partnerships and qualified finance only after permitting and eligibility review; no green-bond or carbon-credit outcome is assumed.

Proof gate: grid-code and data-law review, certified metering, lifecycle emissions evidence and local tariff approval.

03 / MATURE APAC

Continuity and local fit

Facilities and industry

Investigate scenario planning for emergency power and high-density facility loads.

District and residential

Investigate district-level visibility for EV charging and community services without control promises.

Hardware and offtake

Explore consortium procurement and insurer input; neither disaster immunity nor insurance savings is established.

Proof gate: disaster-safety testing, utility and municipal partners, localization quality and support coverage.

04 / EMERGING MARKETS

Access and maintainability

Industrial and SMB

Investigate modular power and compute options for sites with constrained infrastructure.

Community and household

Investigate affordable education and agricultural information where energy and connectivity are reliable enough.

Hardware and offtake

Evaluate development-finance, grant and cooperative models with local partners; no subsidy, tariff or community ownership structure is approved.

Proof gate: site economics, local maintenance, consumer protection, affordable hardware and licensed payment partners.

Commercial hypotheses

None is available for purchase

COMPUTE + ENERGY BUNDLE

Separate, measurable obligations

Explore a combined contract only after energy delivery, compute usage, service levels, taxes and failure remedies can each be measured and governed.

VALUE SHARING

Regulated market participation

Grid export, virtual-power-plant participation or energy revenue sharing would require utility agreements, market permissions and financial controls. No yield or bill reduction is promised.

Hardware and finance feasibility

Site-by-site diligence before any offer

CONTAINERIZED DEPLOYMENT

From concept to permitted site

A modular node would need land rights, generation and storage sizing, cooling, fire and electrical safety, utility studies, transport, maintenance and insurance. A 48-hour deployment or zero utility hookup cannot be assumed.

OFFTAKE + COMMUNITY UTILITY

Contracts are not financing

Take-or-pay, developer bundles, cooperative ownership and community tariffs are possible structures for legal and lender review. None creates a guaranteed return, lower debt cost or approved customer charge by itself.

Dual-footprint asset study

20ft and 40ft are design hypotheses, not product specifications

20FT / TACTICAL MICRO-POD

Smaller-site feasibility

Study constrained campuses, telecom sites and temporary operations only where power, storage, cooling, transport and safety calculations support the load. Critical facilities need their own certification and continuity plans. Equipment leasing or venture debt would require accepted collateral, cash-flow and warranty terms.

40FT / STRATEGIC NODE

Longer-horizon feasibility

Study industrial and planned-community sites with measured energy and compute demand. Infrastructure debt would require permits, construction risk allocation, reliable offtake, service reserves and lender diligence. A 10–20 year tenor or community-scale capacity cannot be assumed.

The energy asset and compute service need separate cost, risk and revenue models. Venture valuation, tax depreciation, flat token prices, developer financing and community subscriptions are not committed. Neither footprint bypasses municipal permission, grid rules or site preparation.

Technology evidence boundary

Research is not a validated integrated system

PHOTOVOLTAIC RESEARCH

Material to certified module

A selenium interlayer is one researched route to multi-junction solar cells. No Ad Astra module efficiency, field yield, supply right or certified array has been verified.

STORAGE RESEARCH

Chemistry to qualified plant

Nickel–hydrogen has aerospace history, but the exact terrestrial battery, pressure-vessel safety case, degradation and service life remain unverified. A separate hydrogen fuel-cell loop would need its own design and energy balance.

Non-negotiable boundary

Ad Astra has not demonstrated off-grid operation, blackout prevention, zero-emission compute, zero latency, autonomous trading, personal superintelligence or disaster-proof facilities. Project financing and hardware availability have not been independently verified. No hospital, military, municipal or residential utility service is offered. AI may inform an operator, but safety-critical energy control requires certified equipment, licensed engineering, local approvals and tested human override.
